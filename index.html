Computational Electromagnetics & Numerical Simulation

Compact accelerators, applied magnetism, antennas & radar — physics-based modelling and experimental validation.

I am Dr. Nezah Balal, Senior Lecturer in Electrical and Electronics Engineering at Ariel University and Head of the Applied Magnetic Systems Laboratory, working on high-frequency electromagnetics, millimeter-wave radar and applied magnetics. This page collects research projects in compact particle accelerators, permanent-magnet undulators, antenna design, and millimeter-wave radar — modeled and analyzed in COMSOL Multiphysics and MATLAB (alongside CST and HFSS), with selected designs experimentally validated.

Research projects

Compact, high-gradient accelerators & IFEL →

Inverse free-electron-laser acceleration with tapered helical undulators, modeled in COMSOL — high-gradient, MeV-scale energy gain over centimeters.

Applied magnetism & undulators →

Permanent-magnet (NdFeB) helical undulators and magnet systems — magnetostatic field maps, harmonics, and tolerance studies.

Antenna design →

Horn, UWB and millimeter-wave antennas and waveguide mode converters — full-wave COMSOL modeling with experimental validation.

Millimeter-wave & micro-Doppler radar →

94 GHz radar systems for remote sensing and target classification, with MATLAB signal processing and machine learning.

Magnetic-mirror traps & particle dynamics →

Electron confinement between two magnetic lenses — Boris-pusher tracing in an ideal thin-loop coil field, mirror condition and loss cone, plus a live interactive 3D simulation.

Frequently asked questions

Who is Dr. Nezah Balal?
Dr. Nezah Balal is a Senior Lecturer in Electrical and Electronics Engineering at Ariel University, Israel, and Head of the Applied Magnetic Systems Laboratory. His research covers millimeter-wave and micro-Doppler radar, contactless human sensing, antenna design, applied magnetics and compact particle accelerators.
What is his research on radar-based human sensing?
His group demonstrated real-time detection of speech-related throat vibration with a 94 GHz micro-Doppler radar (Remote Sensing, 2025), non-imaging fall detection (Applied Sciences, 2022), and time-frequency signatures of limb movements (Sensors, 2020). Ongoing work extends this to contactless vital-signs monitoring.
